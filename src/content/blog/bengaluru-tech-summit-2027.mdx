Bengaluru has been home for quite some time now, and somehow, I've never attended the Bengaluru Tech Summit. Strange, isn't it? Especially when you work in technology and live in a city that's practically synonymous with India's startup ecosystem.

Well, that's about to change! 🚀

I'll be attending **Bengaluru Tech Summit 2027 as a delegate**, and I'm genuinely looking forward to it.

I've heard quite a few good things about the summit, particularly the startup ecosystem, the people you get to meet, and the kind of conversations that happen beyond the conference halls. This will be my first time experiencing it all firsthand, so I'm going in with a fair bit of curiosity and excitement.

## A Little About What I'm Looking Forward To

Most of my professional journey has revolved around cloud infrastructure, virtualization, automation, and developer platforms. Over the years, I've had opportunities to work with some fascinating technologies, but one thing I've come to appreciate is that you can learn quite a lot by stepping outside your usual technology circles.

And I think that's what makes events like these interesting.

I'm attending as a delegate, which means I have the flexibility to explore, attend learning sessions, participate in workshops, and hopefully spend a good amount of time interacting with people who are building interesting things.

I'm particularly looking forward to the technical sessions and hands-on workshops. There's something about seeing a technology in action, asking questions, and understanding how people are actually using it that you simply don't get from reading documentation or watching a recorded presentation.

Of course, the startup showcases are high on my list as well. Bengaluru has an incredible startup ecosystem, and I'm curious to see what people are building, what problems they're trying to solve, and how they're approaching them.

## Private Cloud, Developer Experience, and a Bit of AI

There are a few topics that I'd be particularly happy to have conversations around.

☁️ **Private Cloud and Cloud Infrastructure** are naturally close to my heart. I've spent a good part of my career working in this space, and it's interesting to see how the conversation around private cloud continues to evolve, particularly with AI becoming an important part of enterprise infrastructure.

🛠️ **Developer Experience** is another area that interests me quite a lot. APIs, SDKs, automation, developer tooling, and the overall experience of building on a platform. I strongly believe that even the most sophisticated technology can struggle with adoption if developers find it difficult to work with.

🤖 And then there's **Private AI, Local AI, and Small Language Models (SLMs)**.

This is an area I've been spending more time exploring lately.

Much of the AI conversation today revolves around larger models, increasingly powerful GPUs, and massive infrastructure investments. All of that is fascinating, but I think there's another interesting side to this story.

What if you don't need a massive model to solve a relatively small, well-defined business problem?

What if a smaller model, running locally on reasonably affordable hardware, can do the job just as effectively for a specific use case?

I'm particularly interested in understanding how startups and smaller enterprises are approaching these questions. Not every business has the resources to build or operate large-scale AI infrastructure, and perhaps they don't need to.

Of course, smaller models come with their own limitations and trade-offs. That's precisely what makes this space worth exploring.

I'm hoping to come across some interesting implementations, learn from people experimenting with these technologies, and understand where local AI is actually delivering value beyond demonstrations and proof-of-concepts.
